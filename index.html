What this is · in plain print

A YSWS for sites that fit a hand

That wheel is a hundred and thirty years of one thumb. The devices changed shape eleven times; the hand holding them never changed at all. Then websites stopped following the hardware — they stayed wide, hover-dependent, and two-handed. Open one on a phone and you're dragging a desktop around with your thumb.

Mobile Friendly is a Hack Club YSWS that fixes that. You ship a website or web app that genuinely works on a phone: responsive layout, buttons you can hit, no horizontal scrolling, usable with one hand. We ship the phone gear that hand is holding — a case, a charger, a power bank, or a shot at a phone grant.

Thirteen to eighteen, any country, and the bill is always zero. Hack Club is paid for by donors, not by the people on the line.

Inspection sheet · what gets checkedrev. 390
  • Responsive down to 320 px, with no horizontal scroll anywhere
  • Touch targets at least 44 px, reachable by a thumb
  • Works one-handed, in low light, on a bad connection
  • No hover-only controls — nothing that needs a mouse
  • Open source, because the code is part of what you ship

Log · five entries

How the call runs

Ln 01
Get a Hack Club account this is the whole registration

Make a free Hack Club account — that's it, that's the signup. No application form, no portfolio review. Being thirteen to eighteen is the only permit required.

Then hit Play the 2026 site up at the top of this page. That button is the door: it signs you in and puts you inside the program. Everything after that happens on the phone.

Ln 02
Start the meter

Hackatime runs while you build. Every tracked hour becomes 1 GB of storage in your virtual phone setup. Nothing else is measured, and there's no penalty for being slow.

Ln 03
Build for a thumb

Responsive layout, targets you can hit, no sideways scrolling, open source. Test it in your hand on a real phone — not in a devtools device frame.

Ln 04
Have it inspected

Send it through FeedEx. A person opens it on a real phone, in a real hand, and marks it against the sheet above. Approved projects go public in the community gallery.

Ln 05
Take delivery two routes · you can be on both

There are two ways the gear reaches you, and they aren't either/or — you can claim from both at once.

Everyday route — no streak needed. Every project you ship earns something small and real: a phone case, a screen protector, a charger, or a bit of accessory grant money. You don't have to build every week or keep a streak alive to stay eligible. Ship one good site and a case turns up for the phone you actually own.

Streak route — build steadily, get the bigger gear. Turn a streak path on and the larger things unlock: power banks, SD cards, and a place in the pool for an actual phone grant. This is the long game, not a weekend.

Either way it's a real parcel shipped to your address with your name on it — not a certificate, not a coupon, not a discount code. And sidequest points are separate from all of this: they buy in-game shop items only, they're never money, and they never turn into money.

Answers in plain print

01What is this, exactly?

A Hack Club YSWS. Ship a website or web app built for a phone; we ship phone gear back. No theme, no required stack, no genre — just the hand test.

02Who can join, and how do I actually get in?

Anyone thirteen to eighteen, anywhere on earth. Make a free Hack Club account, then press Play the 2026 site on this page — that button is the entry point. The Slack is for help and showing off, not for getting a foot in the door.

03What does it cost?

Nothing. Joining, building, submitting and receiving are all free. Donors cover it.

04What counts as mobile friendly?

Responsive layout, touch targets big enough to hit with a thumb, no horizontal scroll, usable one-handed — and open source, because the code is part of what you ship.

05How is my time counted?

Hackatime. One tracked hour becomes 1 GB of storage in your virtual phone setup. It's automatic, and it's the only clock we keep.

06Where do I submit?

FeedEx, the official submission app. It's also where you watch the status.

07Where do finished projects go?

The community gallery, publicly, once approved. Someone else's first mobile layout is the best tutorial there is.

08What gear can I get, and do I need a streak?

You don't need a streak to get something. Every shipped project can earn cases, screen protectors, chargers and small accessory grants. A streak path on top of that unlocks the bigger gear: power banks, SD cards and phone grants. Nothing is a certificate or a discount code — it's a parcel with your name on it.

09Where do I ask for help?

#mobile-friendly for general chat, #mobile-friendly-help for a bug you can't kill. Invite at slack.hackclub.com.

10What is PinkKit?

The FAQ app inside your virtual phone. Reddit-shaped, pink, comment threads, upvoting. Same questions, more arguing.

11What are sidequests and points?

Random challenges appear in your virtual setup and pay points you spend in the in-game shop. In-game only — points are not money and never become money.

12Can I turn the pop-ups off?

Yes. There's a setting. No modal deserves to interrupt a layout that finally works.
